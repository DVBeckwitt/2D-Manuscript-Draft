\subsection{Mosaicity}
\label{sec:mosaicity}

Mosaicity is the out-of-plane orientational spread of the layered crystallites about the mean film normal illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:intro_2x3}(f--h). It is distinct from the random in-plane rotation that defines the two-dimensional powder limit. Random in-plane rotation generates an $m$-indexed rod family at a common in-plane reciprocal-space radius $Q_R$, whereas mosaicity spreads each member over crystallite tilt. On an area detector, random azimuthal rotation generates paired off-specular arcs for $m\neq0$. Out-of-plane tilt broadens those arcs and distributes $m=0$ intensity in the transverse detector direction.

Figure~\ref{fig:hk0_low_l_star} shows the low-$L$, $m=0$ region of a Bi$_2$Se$_3$ detector image collected at $\theta_i=5^\circ$. The $006$ feature occurs at approximately twice the radial $2\theta$ coordinate of $003$. The $003$ feature is strongly anisotropic. Its long extension follows the radial $2\theta$ direction, whereas its transverse width follows the detector-plane azimuth $\phi$. These two directions separate the dominant effects of wavelength dispersion and crystallite tilt.

Wavelength sampling changes the magnitude of the incident wavevector and therefore moves the wavelength-specific Ewald intersection along $2\theta$. In the common-scale $m=0$ calculation, the full wavelength distribution reproduces the enhanced $003$ signal and its radial extension. Replacing that distribution by the mean wavelength, while holding the geometry, structure factors, orientation density, background treatment, and detector mapping fixed, collapses the radial streak. The lower-order $003$ feature is especially visible because it lies closer to the strong low-$Q_z$ specular response, so the wavelength convolution samples a higher-intensity radial envelope than it does around $006$. The recorded $003$/$006$ contrast is therefore not determined by the two structure factors alone. We assign the extended radial component of $003$ primarily to wavelength dispersion.

The transverse $\phi$ width behaves differently. It remains after the wavelength distribution is collapsed and changes with the crystallite-orientation distribution. The $\phi$ extent is therefore the cleaner constraint on mosaicity. The bright near-origin intensity belongs to the near-critical specular regime and is treated separately in Sec.~\ref{sec:reflectivity}. It is not used as evidence for the broad mosaic component.

The off-specular rod-family arcs and the transverse $\phi$ extent of off-condition $m=0$ features sample different angular ranges of the same orientation distribution. Strong off-specular arcs predominantly constrain crystallites close to the mean film normal. Weak transverse $m=0$ intensity away from the nominal specular direction is more sensitive to the low-probability population at larger tilt. The radial $003$ streak is not used for that inference because it is reproduced by the wavelength distribution. We represent the two mosaic scales with the compact phenomenological density
\begin{equation}
  \omega_{\mathrm w}(\Delta\theta;p_{\mathrm{tail}},\Gamma,\sigma)
  =p_{\mathrm{tail}} L_{\mathrm w}(\Delta\theta;\Gamma)
  +(1-p_{\mathrm{tail}})G_{\mathrm w}(\Delta\theta;\sigma),
  \qquad 0\le p_{\mathrm{tail}} \le 1 .
  \label{eq:mosaic_two_component_maintext}
\end{equation}
Here $G_{\mathrm w}$ is the Gaussian-like narrow core, $L_{\mathrm w}$ is the Lorentzian-like broad component, $p_{\mathrm{tail}}$ is its weight, and $\Delta\theta$ is crystallite tilt relative to the mean layer normal. The two widths are independent and share one center. The Lorentzian-like form is a convenient broad-tailed parameterization, not a claim that the physical distribution is uniquely Lorentzian. The present overlays show that this two-component form can represent the selected profiles. Establishing necessity requires both a fixed-parameter no-tail counterfactual and a fairly re-optimized Gaussian-only comparison.

Figure~\ref{fig:mosaic_combo} connects the two parts of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mosaic_two_component_maintext} to reciprocal-space observables. In panels~(a) and~(b), the compact purple segment marks the narrow core and the dashed orange extension marks the low-probability broad component. For $m\neq0$, the strong detector arcs primarily sample the compact region near the nominal reciprocal vector. For $m=0$, intensity displaced transversely from the film-normal direction requires larger crystallite tilts and is therefore more sensitive to the broad component. The same angular density generates both effects. The radial coordinate is folded onto $Q_R\geq0$, so opposite signed in-plane tilt components map to the same radial locus.
