\section{Ordered Bi\texorpdfstring{$_2$}{2}Se\texorpdfstring{$_3$}{3} and Bi\texorpdfstring{$_2$}{2}Te\texorpdfstring{$_3$}{3} films}
\label{sec:results_ordered}

Detector images collected at incident angles of $5^\circ$, $10^\circ$, and $15^\circ$ entered the staged refinement of the ordered Bi$_2$Se$_3$ and Bi$_2$Te$_3$ films. Figures~\ref{fig:ordered_bi2se3_test} and~\ref{fig:ordered_bi2te3_test} show the nominal $5^\circ$ condition, which displays both off-specular diffraction and the axial $r=0$ response within one detector image. Each material uses the same layout: a measured-minus-background and simulated detector pair above the selected rod profiles.

The off-axis profile rows show the two detector-side intersections of the $r=1$, $r=3$, and $r=4$ families against projected $L_{\mathrm{proj}}$. Plus and minus distinguish the two sides of each family. The axial $r=0$ row uses detector $2\theta$. Dark points and connecting lines denote data minus background, and gray bands show the labeled data uncertainty. Pale gray regions lie outside the comparison interval. Blue dashed vertical guides identify ideal Bragg positions. The profile scales retain zero and negative background-subtracted values. Supporting Information Sec.~S4 defines the shared finite-region observable, and Sec.~S6 reserves the direct comparisons and additional incident conditions.

The off-axis and axial features provide complementary observables. Off-axis peak positions constrain the two-dimensional-powder construction and calibrated detector geometry. Their widths and asymmetries depend on mosaicity, source spread, and finite integration width. In the axial family, radial $2\theta$ structure depends on finite-stack intensity, orientation selection, the source distribution, and the optical response. The transverse $\phi_f$ width provides a separate crystallite-tilt observable.

\subsection{Continuous rod intensity and Ewald selection}
\label{sec:continuous_rod_bridge}

The constant-$|\mathbf Q|$ construction describes one Bragg position, whereas a finite film also scatters between these positions. Figure~\ref{fig:continuous_rod_bridge} extends this construction to the continuous rods of Sec.~\ref{sec:structure_factor}. Random in-plane orientations distribute each off-axis rod over a cylinder. Mosaic tilts rotate the cylinder and its intensity about the reciprocal origin. The Ewald condition selects the contributions compatible with elastic scattering.

  \centering
  \captionsetup{font=footnotesize}
  \includegraphics[width=5.5in]{figures/geometry/cylinder_bridge_journal.pdf}
  \caption[Continuous rod scattering and Ewald selection]{From Bragg spheres to continuous powder cylinders and Ewald-selected scattering. (a) Random in-plane azimuth spreads Bragg points into purple rings at crystallite-frame coordinates $L=1,2,3$. Each lies on a constant-$|\mathbf Q|$ sphere about the reciprocal origin $O$, shown in gray for $L=1$. Dashed guides connect equal-radius points. (b) Finite thickness gives continuous rod intensity. Azimuthal averaging sweeps one rod (dark teal) into a cylinder with the same intensity profile. The black curve shows the peak-normalized interference factor of eight identical, equally spaced layers, $S_8(L)=|\sum_{n=0}^{7}\exp(2\pi i nL)|^2/64$, for the representative $r=1$ family. (c) Mosaic tilts rotate cylinders and intensity profiles together about $O$. Gray lines indicate crystallite normals. Teal curves mark intersections with the fixed pale-blue Ewald sphere. (d) The crystallite-normal distribution weights the intersections, which combine into a scattering pattern on the sphere. Gray dashed curves show the aligned-cylinder reference. Blue $\mathbf k_i$ and orange $\mathbf k_f$ originate at $-\mathbf k_i$. The orange point satisfies $\mathbf k_f=\mathbf k_i+\mathbf Q$, with $|\mathbf k_f|=|\mathbf k_i|=2\pi/\lambda$. Dimensionless $L$ follows the crystallite normal, while $Q_z$ is the component along the mean film normal. Stacking disorder modifies rod intensity within the same geometry.}
  \label{fig:continuous_rod_bridge}
\end{figure}

Stacking correlations determine how intensity is distributed along the rods. Changing those correlations therefore extends the same orientation and Ewald construction to the diffuse scattering in PbI$_2$, discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:results_pbi2}.
