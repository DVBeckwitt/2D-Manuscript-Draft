\subsection{Mosaicity, bandwidth, and Bragg-sphere size}
\label{sec:correlated_effects}

The observed intensities are controlled by the overlap of wavelength bandwidth, mosaic orientation, and incident-angle phase space. For the $m=0$, $00L$ family, mosaicity populates a cap-like region of the Bragg sphere, and the measured intensity is the portion of the finite-bandwidth Ewald intersection that falls inside that cap. Because $G_{00L}=2\pi L/c$, a fixed reciprocal-space Ewald-shell thickness maps to an angular band width that scales approximately as $1/L$ after projection onto the Bragg sphere. Thus the same bandwidth samples a larger angular fraction of low-$L$ Bragg spheres than high-$L$ Bragg spheres, allowing low-order peaks to retain mosaic overlap over larger incident-angle detuning. The reciprocal-space scaling is derived in the Supporting Information.

This geometry explains a counterintuitive feature of the measurements. Increasing the incidence angle can bring high-order $00L$ peaks into condition while the low-order peaks remain visible, but lowering the incidence angle does not necessarily preserve the high-order peaks. The low-order peaks are robust because the finite Ewald-shell thickness and the mosaic cap continue to overlap. The high-order peaks require a more specific intersection because their Bragg spheres are larger and the same bandwidth samples a smaller fraction of the populated cap.
