\subsection{Incident-beam phase space and wavelength}
\label{sec:correlated_effects}
\label{sec:refraction}
\label{sec:bandwidth_mosaic_cap}

The incident beam is represented by weighted states in position, propagation direction, and wavelength. Position and direction determine where the ray reaches the film and its local incidence condition. Wavelength fixes the magnitude of the incident wavevector and therefore the Ewald-sphere construction used for that state. These variables are inputs to one forward calculation rather than corrections applied after a detector image has been formed.

For wavelength state $\lambda_j$ with spectral weight $w_j$,
\begin{equation}
  k_0(\lambda_j)=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_j},
  \qquad
  \mathbf k_i(\lambda_j)=k_0(\lambda_j)\hat{\mathbf s}_{i,j},
  \qquad
  \left|\mathbf k_i(\lambda_j)+\mathbf G\right|=k_0(\lambda_j).
  \label{eq:moving_ewald_condition_main}
\end{equation}
With the reciprocal-space origin fixed, the Ewald sphere for state $j$ is centered at $-\mathbf k_i(\lambda_j)$ and has radius $k_0(\lambda_j)$.
\cite{AlsNielsenMcMorrow2001} Changing wavelength therefore changes both the center and the radius. The full vector condition in Eq.~\eqref{eq:moving_ewald_condition_main} is evaluated for every weighted direction and wavelength state, and the resulting detector intensities are summed incoherently. The source-state construction and convergence checks are given in the Supporting Information.

The $003$ feature provides a direct detector-space test of this wavelength integration. The complete calculation uses the retained wavelength distribution. A control calculation replaces that distribution by its mean wavelength while leaving the geometry, structure factors, continuous mosaic density, background treatment, and detector mapping unchanged. The complete calculation reproduces the extended radial $2\theta$ signal through $003$, whereas the monochromatic control collapses that extension. The transverse $\phi$ width is retained because it is set primarily by the crystallite-orientation distribution. This controlled comparison identifies wavelength dispersion as the dominant origin of the radial streak and separates it from the mosaic-sensitive transverse width.

Standard planar-interface refraction and attenuation corrections are applied to the incident and exit fields before an accepted contribution is mapped to the detector. Their influence is strongest when an incident or exit angle approaches the critical-angle regime and becomes less dominant as the propagation angles move away from that region. These corrections are included for physical completeness but are not a principal result of this work. Their boundary-condition equations, branch convention, attenuation integral, and wavelength dependence are given in the Supporting Information.

\subsection{Specular response from reflectivity to diffraction}
\label{sec:reflectivity}

The bright near-origin $m=0$ intensity in Fig.~\ref{fig:hk0_low_l_star} belongs to the near-critical specular regime. Its physical origin is distinct from the wavelength-broadened $003$ streak and from the transverse orientational broadening used to constrain mosaicity. At low $Q_z$, refraction, evanescence, absorption, roughness, and multiple reflection at the film interfaces are described by a standard Parratt recursion.
\cite{Parratt1954} At higher $Q_z$, including the $003$ Bragg region, the finite-stack kinematic structure term describes the diffraction response. We refer to the smooth matching of these two limiting descriptions as the \emph{Parratt-to-kinematic crossover}.

Figure~\ref{fig:specular_joint_fit_placeholder} reserves the main-text comparison that will place the low-$Q_z$ reflectivity region and the $003$ peak on one detector-derived profile. The comparison uses one coordinate transformation, one background treatment, and one intensity scale across both regions. The expanded $003$ panel will also show the full wavelength calculation and the monochromatic control so that the radial wavelength effect is visible independently of the near-critical optical response. The detailed Parratt recursion and crossover construction are given in the Supporting Information.
