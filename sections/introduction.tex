\section{Introduction}
Layered van der Waals solids are often prepared as thin films, where their anisotropic crystal structures make crystallite orientation and phase central to the measured response.\cite{GeimGrigorieva2013,Jerng2013,Zhang2022Bi2Te3} Depending on the substrate and growth conditions, the crystallites may align their layer normals with the film normal while retaining little common in-plane azimuthal registry.\cite{Zhang2022Bi2Te3} We refer to this uniaxially textured limit as a two-dimensional (2D) powder: individual crystallites remain three-dimensional, but one crystallographic axis is preferentially aligned while their rotations about it are approximately random.\cite{Kaganer1999,Fischer2023MOFGIWAXS} The resulting reciprocal-space distribution produces characteristic specular and off-specular features that an area detector records simultaneously, providing access to their detector-space positions, apparent line shapes, azimuthal extents, and intensities for corrected quantitative comparison.\cite{SmilgiesBlasini2007,Hexemer2015,Savikhin2020}

The difficulty is that the same detector features can arise from several correlated experimental and sample-dependent causes, including finite wavelength bandwidth, beam divergence, grazing-incidence refraction, sample misalignment, mosaic spread, and disorder.\cite{Kaganer1999,Breiby2008,Savikhin2020,Smilgies2025Crystals} One solution is a forward model that starts from a crystallographic and structural hypothesis and propagates it through the incident beam distribution, sample geometry, scattering kinematics, calibrated detector mapping, and finite detector integration. Keeping the calculation in detector space allows the assumptions that generate each feature to be tested against the measured two-dimensional intensity distribution rather than only a one-dimensional reduction. Figure~\ref{fig:intro_2x3} places the 2D-powder state between the single-crystal and 3D-powder limits in real, reciprocal, and detector space.

Figure~\ref{fig:intro_2x3} summarizes the real-space, reciprocal-space, and detector-space consequences of these three orientational limits. Here $\mathbf{k}_i$ and $\mathbf{k}_f$ are the incident and exit wavevectors, and $\mathbf{G}$ is a reciprocal-lattice vector. With this convention, the Ewald sphere is centered at $-\mathbf{k}_i$, passes through the reciprocal-space origin, and has radius $|\mathbf{k}_i|=2\pi/\lambda$. Elastic diffraction occurs where reciprocal-space intensity satisfies $|\mathbf{k}_i+\mathbf{G}|=|\mathbf{k}_i|$. Each allowed point defines an exit direction $\mathbf{k}_f=\mathbf{k}_i+\mathbf{G}$.\cite{AlsNielsenMcMorrow2001} For a single crystal, reciprocal space consists of isolated Bragg points [Fig.~\ref{fig:intro_2x3}(b)]. Only isolated sphere--point crossings produce detector spots, so the detector pattern is sparse. For a 3D powder, random crystallite orientations spread each reflection over a spherical shell [Fig.~\ref{fig:intro_2x3}(d)]. The sphere--shell intersection is a circle that maps to the Debye--Scherrer ring in Fig.~\ref{fig:intro_2x3}(e).\cite{AlsNielsenMcMorrow2001} For a 2D powder, azimuthal disorder about the common $c$ axis spreads each off-specular Bragg point into a ring about the film-normal reciprocal-space axis, while specular $(00L)$ intensity remains near that axis [Fig.~\ref{fig:intro_2x3}(g)].\cite{Kaganer1999,SmilgiesBlasini2007} The Ewald sphere generally cuts an off-specular ring at two points, which map to paired detector features when both directions fall within detector acceptance [Fig.~\ref{fig:intro_2x3}(h)].\cite{SmilgiesBlasini2007,Hexemer2015,Smilgies2005Rods} Mosaicity then broadens these ideal intersections into arcs and cap-like intensity rather than changing the basic diffraction condition.\cite{Kaganer1999,SmilgiesBlasini2007}

This hybrid detector-space information is what makes the 2D-powder problem both rich and difficult. Many standard WAXS workflows reduce area-detector images to 1D profiles by azimuthal integration, often with masking near the direct beam and specular region.\cite{Ashiotis2015,Hexemer2015,Strzalka2018,Reus2024,Savikhin2020} Those steps suit nearly isotropic powders, but for 2D powders they deweight or remove off-specular arcs and specular caps that constrain out-of-plane correlations, stacking, and mosaic tails.\cite{SmilgiesBlasini2007,Hexemer2015} Stacking disorder is also commonly inferred from 1D interference models and refinements,\cite{HendricksTeller1942,Treacy1991,CasasCabanas2016} which do not directly exploit the two-dimensional anisotropy of oriented-film scattering. More general intermediate orientation distributions can be treated with orientation-distribution-function (ODF) refinements or preferred-orientation corrections,\cite{March1932,Dollase1986,MatthiesVinel1982,Pawlik1986,VonDreele1997,LutterottiVasinWenk2014} but those approaches are more general than needed for the narrowly constrained 2D-powder case considered here.\cite{VonDreele1997,LutterottiVasinWenk2014} Recent radial-profile approaches have made thin-film GIXD substantially more quantitative, but peak overlap and background separation can remain limiting in strongly anisotropic patterns, especially at small incidence angles.\cite{Gasser2025} For the layered films considered here, the measured morphology already motivates axial symmetry about the film normal and a low-dimensional out-of-plane mosaicity kernel.\cite{Kaganer1999,Smilgies2000POPOP,Fischer2023MOFGIWAXS} We therefore assume this physically motivated 2D-powder orientation distribution a priori and refine it with a detector-space intensity forward model, using off-specular arcs and specular-family line shapes as direct constraints.\cite{SmilgiesBlasini2007,Hexemer2015}

Prior work has established the main ingredients needed for such a framework, including grazing-incidence scattering formalisms,\cite{Sinha1988,Renaud2009} reciprocal-space mapping and indexing for oriented thin films,\cite{SmilgiesBlasini2007,Smilgies2005Rods,Hexemer2015,Savikhin2020} detector-space visualization and indexing of GIXS/GIWAXS patterns,\cite{SmilgiesLi2026IndexGIXS} and forward calculations of diffraction from textured films.\cite{Breiby2008} Most directly, Smilgies and Li calculated grazing-incidence diffraction-spot positions in detector space rather than nonlinearly transforming the full detector image into reciprocal space.\cite{SmilgiesLi2026IndexGIXS} The present work extends that geometric starting point to a continuous detector-coordinate intensity calculation that combines the incident beam phase space, calibrated sample and detector geometry, a continuous crystallite-orientation distribution, structure-factor weighting, and finite detector integration. Standard planar-interface refraction and attenuation corrections are included where required, but they are not a principal contribution of this work.

Here we present the resulting detector-space intensity forward model for WAXS from 2D-powder films and apply the same framework to ordered Bi$_2$Se$_3$ and Bi$_2$Te$_3$ films.\cite{Jerng2013,Zhang2022Bi2Te3} Figures~\ref{fig:ordered_bi2se3_test} and~\ref{fig:ordered_bi2te3_test} show the $\theta_i=5^\circ$ condition, where calculated hexagonal rod-family trajectories track selected measured Bragg-feature locations and the corresponding projected profiles reproduce the principal peak positions and apparent line shapes. Images collected at $5^\circ$, $10^\circ$, and $15^\circ$ entered the staged refinement. The displayed $5^\circ$ comparisons therefore show agreement for a condition used in that refinement rather than an independent incidence-angle prediction. The result is a direct detector-space comparison that retains anisotropic information obscured by a conventional one-dimensional reduction.

The paper first develops the diffraction geometry and detector-space intensity calculation, then uses the measured low-$L$ features and their reciprocal-space geometry to motivate the mosaicity model. The incident beam phase space and wavelength dependence are introduced as inputs to the same calculation, while standard planar-interface corrections and the near-critical specular diagnostic are documented chiefly in the Supporting Information. The ordered Bi$_2$Se$_3$ and Bi$_2$Te$_3$ test cases are followed by the staged refinement workflow used to construct those comparisons. PbI$_2$ is treated afterward as a separate stacking-disorder extension that retains the detector-space projection while changing the structure factor along fixed in-plane-momentum trajectories. The discussion closes by relating the intensity calculation to detector-space indexing and structural interpretation.
